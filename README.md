# Background

Generative AI is spreading at an unprecedented pace: ChatGPT reached an estimated 100 million monthly users just two months after launch, the fastest ramp of any consumer app on record,[^1] while the internet took four years after opening to the public to reach 50 million users[^2]—an acceleration that will fundamentally rework how research is conceived, conducted, and communicated.

# BYU AI Association

The [BYU AI Association](https://aia.byu.edu/) (AIA) exists to bridge the gap between classroom learning and industry demands. The group is multidisciplinary and discusses all aspects of artificial intelligence.

# AI in Research Chapter

This repository proposes an AI in Research chapter of the BYU AIA: an association specifically
for undergraduate and graduate researchers at BYU, starting with the College of Physical and
Mathematical Sciences (CPMS) and the College of Life Sciences.

Faculty in the CPMS now set aside time to experiment with AI, adapt it into their courses, and meet weekly to learn
from one another. This chapter will give student researchers the same opportunity, focused specifically
on their research.

The chapter will encourage students to take part in hackathons, networking events and the other
activities organized by the BYU AIA. In addition, it will hold its own meetings focused on student
research and collaboration between labs.

The chapter will keep a private GitHub repository where members share tools, workflows and AI tips.
Any member will be given access on request.

## Goals

1. Share how student researchers are using AI in their labs.
2. Train members in practical AI tools for research.
3. Keep members current on AI developments that affect science and their field of research.
4. Connect students with faculty and professionals who use AI in research.
5. Maintain a shared collection of tools, workflows and projects, open to all members.
6. Promote ethical and privacy-conscious use of AI in research.

## Meetings

**Thursdays, 6:00 pm (time tentative).** Each meeting will open with AI news, followed by a training,
discussion or guest speaker. Students from different labs will be asked to present their research
and how they use artificial intelligence, with each lab assigned its own week to present. Members
are also encouraged to attend the AI Association's Wednesday meetings.
